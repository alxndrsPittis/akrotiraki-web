Sifnos

Food

Sifnos has a name for its cooking: slow dishes baked in clay pots, cheese matured in wine, thyme honey and almond sweets.

Products and dishes

Revithada
Chickpeas with onion, bay leaf and olive oil, put into the wood oven on Saturday in a lidded clay pot, the skepastaria, and baked overnight for Sunday lunch.
Mastelo
Lamb or goat with red wine and dill, slow-cooked in the clay pot that gives it its name; an Easter dish.
Revithokeftedes and kaparosalata
Chickpea fritters, and a dip of dried capers blended with onion and herbs. You will find both on many menus.
Manoura
A cheese of goat’s and sheep’s milk, matured in the lees of red wine, which colour its rind; it grows sharper with age. Look also for xinomyzithra, a soft sour cheese.
Honey and sweets
Thyme honey, harvested in August; pasteli, bars of sesame and honey; melopita, the honey and cheese pie of Easter; and amygdalota, almond sweets shaped by hand with rosewater.
Capers
Caper bushes grow wild out of the stone walls. The buds and leaves are sold salted, in jars, to take home.

Nikolaos Tselementes, born in Exambela in 1878, published his cookbook in the early 1930s; his name became a Greek word for a cookbook. The Cultural Association of Sifnos holds a gastronomy festival in his name, usually in September.
