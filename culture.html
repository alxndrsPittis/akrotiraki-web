Sifnos

Culture

Pottery, music and the summer feasts of Sifnos.

Pottery

Sifnos has a long tradition of pottery. Its clay, formed from eroded shale, withstands fire, and the island’s cooking pots are made from it: the tsikali for stews, the skepastaria for revithada, the mastelo for slow-cooked meat, the flaros chimney pot, as well as jugs, jars and braziers.

In the centuries of piracy the workshops stood inland, at Artemonas and Ano Petali. When piracy ended, the potters moved down to the shore, where boats could load their wares, and lived at the workshop through the week. From the 18th and 19th centuries many left to work elsewhere, in Attica and on other islands, and in Greek the word Sifnios came to mean a potter. Sifnian pottery is on Greece’s National Inventory of Intangible Cultural Heritage, and fifteen to twenty workshops are active.

Music and dance

Traditional music on Sifnos is played by the takimi, the local pairing of violin and laouto, at the panigyria and local festivals. The dances are those of the Cyclades, among them the syrtos and the balos; most of the songs are love songs. A poem by Aristomenis Provelengios, a poet born on Sifnos, «Αέρι», was set to music and is still sung.

Recordings: Songs of Sifnos, unpublished recordings 1930–1998 gathers 21 songs from the Melpo Merlier Music Folklore Archive and other collections.

Where to hear it:

  • At the panigyria, below, through the summer.
  • MuSifanto, a classical music festival in late June and early July, held in monastery courtyards, village squares and on old paths; its closing nights have included island violin and laouto. filoitounisiou.gr/musifanto.
  • The Nikolaos Tselementes gastronomy festival in September, in the square at Artemonas, with violin and laouto and dancing every evening.
  • The Cultural Association’s dance groups, who perform traditional dances on Whit Monday.

Panigyria

A panigyri is the feast of a church on its saint’s day, religious and social at once: vespers on the eve, then a meal served to everyone present, with music and dancing.

On Sifnos each feast is hosted and paid for by a panigyras, a local person, a family or a group of friends and donors, not by the municipality or by sponsors. The panigyras keeps the saint’s icon at home for a year and then hands it on; some churches have waiting lists of years. The meal follows custom: revithada first, then meat in red sauce with pasta, or cod in fasting periods, and bread for everyone present.

The panigyria follow the church calendar, from Ascension (the feast of Chrysopigi) to mid-September; dates are announced locally each year, and a calendar is kept here.

Museums and monasteries

Folklore Museum, Apollonia
On the main square: Sifnian lace, costumes and straw hats, tools, cooking pots, and an early edition of Tselementes’ cookbook. It closed for a time in 2024, so ask us whether it is open.
Chrysopigi
The 17th-century monastery of Panagia Chrysopigi, patron of the island since 1964, on a split rock in the sea between Platys Gialos and Faros.
Panagia Vrysiani
A 17th-century monastery at Exambela, with a museum of ecclesiastical art.
Profitis Ilias o Psilos
A fortified monastery of the mid-17th century on the island’s highest peak, reached on foot from Fyrogia in an hour or so.
